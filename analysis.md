# TAMIL NADU 2026 ASSEMBLY ELECTION ANALYSIS

### Overview
The 2026 Tamil Nadu Assembly election represented a significant transformation in the state's political landscape. For the first time in decades, the traditional two-party contest between DMK and AIADMK was disrupted by a new entrant that captured substantial electoral support. This report analyzes three interconnected research questions that reveal the nature and extent of this political transformation.

## 1. INTRODUCTION & METHODOLOGY

Tamil Nadu has historically been characterized by a bipolar political contest between the DMK (Dravidian progressive movement) and AIADMK (Tamil maanila munnupal). This configuration, while occasionally disrupted by regional parties, has remained relatively stable for over three decades.

The 2026 election marked a departure from this pattern. For the first time, a completely new entrant captured sufficient electoral share to reshape the political mathematics of the state.

## 2. RESEARCH QUESTION 1: THE VOTE SHARE STORY

**Where did TVK's 34.92% vote share come from? Which established parties lost support?Where did TVK's votes come from? Compare party vote share state-wide and by region in 2021 vs 2026. Did TVK pull from DMK, AIADMK, both, or from previously non-voting populations?**

#### State-Wide Vote Distribution (2021 vs 2026)

| Party | 2021 Vote Share | 2026 Vote Share | Change (pp) | Interpretation  |
|-------|-----------------|-----------------|-------------|-----------------|
| DMK   |      37.7%      |      24.19%     |  -13.51pp   | Lost vote share |
| AIADMK|      33.29%     |      21.21%     |  -12.08pp   | Lost vote share |
| TVK   |      0.0%       |      34.92%     |  +34.92pp   |   NEW ENTRANT   |

- **TVK's vote share:** 34.92% (new party)
- **Combined DMK + AIADMK loss:** 25.59 percentage points

**Interpretation:** TVK's 34.92% vote share almost accounts for the 25.59 percentage point combined loss from DMK and AIADMK, suggesting TVK drew approximately equally from both traditional parties and got other of its percentage vote share from other parties and non voting populations.

This represents a genuine **realignment**, not just growth from one party.

## 5. LIMITATIONS & DATA QUALITY

#### What This Analysis Shows (Strengths)
✓ **Descriptive accuracy:** What changed in vote share and margins
✓ **Geographic patterns:** Regional variations in TVK support
✓ **Comparative analysis:** 2021 vs 2026 differences
✓ **Constituency-level detail:** Seat-by-seat breakdown
✓ **Reproducibility:** Based on official ECI data

#### What This Analysis Cannot Show (Limitations)
❌ **Causation:** Why voters switched (requires surveys)
❌ **Individual voter behavior:** Which types voted for TVK
❌ **Attitude shifts:** Why voter preferences changed
❌ **Campaign effects:** Which parties' campaigns worked
❌ **Welfare programs:** Policy impact on voting
❌ **Media influence:** News coverage effects
❌ **Booth-level detail:** Micro-area voting patterns

#### Constituency-Aggregated Data
This analysis uses constituency-level vote counts. While this is sufficient for state and regional analysis, it:
- Cannot identify which specific voters switched parties
- Cannot isolate individual demographic factors (caste, religion, occupation)
- Cannot determine within-constituency micro-patterns

#### Two-Election Comparison
Comparing only 2021 and 2026 means:
- Cannot identify long-term trends
- Cannot separate cyclical from structural change
- Limited to 5-year interval

#### No Survey Data
This analysis is based entirely on results data. To answer "why" questions would require:
- Exit polls
- Post-election surveys
- Campaign analysis
- Media coverage assessment
